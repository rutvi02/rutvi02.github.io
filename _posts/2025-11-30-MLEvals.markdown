Machine Learning Evaluation (ML Evals) is the systematic process of measuring how well a model performs on a given task. It is the bridge between training a model and deploying it, ensuring that the system is accurate, reliable, and safe for real-world use.

Why ML Evals Matter
Without a robust evaluation framework, deploying AI is guesswork. Proper evals help you:
Measure Generalization: Ensure the model works on new, unseen data, not just what it memorized during training.
Identify Bias: Catch systematic errors that might unfairly impact specific demographic groups.Drive Iteration: Provide a "north star" metric (like Accuracy or F1 Score) to determine if a new model version is truly better.
Ensure Safety: Evals are critical for detecting "hallucinations" or harmful outputs in generative models.

1. Traditional ML Metrics
For standard tasks like classification or predicting prices, we use objective, mathematical formulas.
Metric          Best For...              What it Measures
Accuracy        Balanced datasets         The percentage of total correct predictions.
Precision      Avoiding False Positives   "Of all predicted positives, how many were actually correct?"
Recall         Avoiding False Negatives   "Of all actual positives, how many did we successfully catch?"
F1-Score       Imbalanced datasets        The harmonic mean of Precision and Recall.
RMSERegression  The average magnitude of error (penalizes large errors heavily).The Confusion Matrix is the foundational tool for these metrics, showing a grid of True Positives, True Negatives, False Positives, and False Negatives.

2. Modern LLM Evaluation
Evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) is harder because there isn't always one "correct" answer. Modern evals have shifted toward more subjective and open-ended methods:
LLM-as-a-Judge: Using a highly capable model (like Gemini 1.5 Pro or GPT-4o) to grade the outputs of a smaller model based on a rubric.Benchmarks: Standardized tests like MMLU (general knowledge) or HumanEval (coding) that allow researchers to compare models on a level playing field.
Human-in-the-loop: The "gold standard" where experts manually review model responses for nuance, tone, and helpfulness.RAG Evals: Specific metrics like Faithfulness (is the answer grounded in the source?) and Answer Relevancy.
